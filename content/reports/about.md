# About this concept

**Group 14:** Sebastian Kumar, Alex Willoughby, Jiya Gupta, Shane Smith

AI systems can now produce research-level mathematics in hours, at a fraction of the traditional cost. This raises open questions about human understanding of mathematical knowledge, the economics of mathematics research, and the wider effects on society.

This site is a concept redesign of arXiv, the preprint server mathematicians use to share their work. It explores one proposed response:

- **Declarations of understanding instead of AI disclosure:** papers don't say which tools they used. Instead, named people declare which results they understand, both the statement and the proof, and are accountable if those results turn out to be wrong or mis-specified.
- **Lean verification:** results can be backed by Lean proofs, re-checked by multiple independent kernel implementations and institutions.
- **Computed understanding levels:** each paper is Level 1 (fully understood), Level 2 (partially understood), or Level 3 (verified only), derived from the declarations and following citations transitively.

This is a mockup for a university ethics course. It is **not affiliated with or endorsed by arXiv or Cornell University**. All papers, authors, reviewers, and links are **synthetic**. The site is fully static and has no backend.
