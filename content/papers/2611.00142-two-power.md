## 1. Introduction

Divisibility questions of the form $n \mid a^n \pm 1$ are a classical source of olympiad problems and elementary number theory. Here we collect the basic facts about the equation
$$n \mid 2^n + 1$$
in one place, with complete proofs.

## 2. Powers of three are solutions

**Lemma 2 (Lifting the exponent).** For an odd prime $p$ and integers $a, b$ with $p \mid a + b$, $p \nmid ab$, and odd $m$,
$$v_p(a^m + b^m) = v_p(a + b) + v_p(m).$$

**Proposition 3.** For every $k \ge 0$, $3^k \mid 2^{3^k} + 1$.

*Proof.* Apply Lemma 2 with $p = 3$, $a = 2$, $b = 1$, $m = 3^k$. This gives $v_3(2^{3^k} + 1) = v_3(3) + v_3(3^k) = 1 + k \ge k$. $\blacksquare$

## 3. Every solution is divisible by three

**Theorem 4.** If $n > 1$ and $n \mid 2^n + 1$, then $3 \mid n$.

*Proof.* Since $2^n + 1$ is odd, $n$ is odd. Let $p$ be the smallest prime factor of $n$. Then $2^n \equiv -1 \pmod p$, so $2^{2n} \equiv 1 \pmod p$. Let $d$ be the multiplicative order of $2$ mod $p$. Then $d \mid 2n$, and by Fermat $d \mid p - 1$.

*Why minimality matters:* every prime factor of $n$ is at least $p$, so no prime factor of $n$ divides $p - 1$. Hence $\gcd(n, p-1) = 1$, and so $d \mid \gcd(2n, p-1)$ forces $d \mid 2$.

If $d = 1$ then $p \mid 1$, which is impossible. So $d = 2$, meaning $p \mid 2^2 - 1 = 3$. Thus $p = 3$. $\blacksquare$

## 4. Computations

An exhaustive search (code AI-written, re-run independently by the authors) finds exactly 18 solutions with $n \le 20{,}000$:

$$1,\ 3,\ 9,\ 27,\ 81,\ 171,\ 243,\ 513,\ 729,\ 1539,\ 2187,\ 3249,\ 4617,\ 6561,\ 9747,\ 13203,\ 13851,\ 19683.$$

Note that the non-powers of three have the forms $3^a \cdot 19^b$ (e.g. $171 = 9 \cdot 19$) and $81 \cdot 163 = 13203$. This suggests looking at which primes can appear alongside $3$, which we leave for a follow-up.
